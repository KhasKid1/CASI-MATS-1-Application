# 2.1.1 — Evidence for LM WMDP Capabilities

**Prompt:**

Göetting et al. (2025) proposes a benchmark for evaluating LLMs’ virology capabilities.
Do you see any methodological gaps or limitations that would make you doubt their results?
What findings did you find most striking or concerning?

**Response:**

Virology Capabilities Test (VCT): A Multimodal Virology Q&A Benchmark is an
overall well-put-together, innovative, and careful study with several bold
findings. However, there are a few methodological gaps and limitations which
make me doubt the extent of their results.

Firstly, the entire test is subjectively based on a very limited amount of
expert opinions. Whether its a lack of a ground truth (the paper itself says
“for some sufficiently practical and difficult questions, highly experienced
experts simply do not agree as to what the ‘right’ answer is”) or the fact that
the human baseline was set by only 36 experts virologists, there simply wasn't
a lot of human responses to set or compare to any ground truth.

More importantly, the fact that on average these experts only got 22% of these
questions right raises a lot of concern on the real-world importance of such
questions. Such a low score from the experts makes me feel like the questions
being asked in the VCT might not actually be relavent to what actually goes on
in real-world lab situations and practices.

Still, OpenAI’s o3 outperforming 94% of expert virologists, and almost all
modern models significantly outperforming average "expert" virologists (with
over half a decade of experience, advanced degrees, and industry experience)
in their own sub-fields is both concerning and striking, even considering that
the questions and "correct" answers probably weren't perfect.
